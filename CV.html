Research

My research is on what happens to people in the years after a myocardial infarction — how much life expectancy is lost, how that can be estimated well enough to be useful in the clinic, and what secondary prevention actually achieves. The work is mostly done in the national quality registries, above all SWEDEHEART, and in population cohorts such as SCAPIS.

Methodologically I work with time-to-event analysis — competing risks, relative survival and loss of life expectancy — along with propensity-score methods, Bayesian survival models and machine-learning approaches to risk prediction. I write the analyses myself, in R and Quarto, under version control.

I co-supervise a doctoral project at Karolinska Institutet on heart failure after myocardial infarction.

I have received research funding from the Swedish Heart-Lung Foundation, ALF Region Stockholm, Region Skåne and the Department of Cardiology at Danderyd University Hospital, and from an industry collaboration with Merck.
